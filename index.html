About

A clinical perspective shaped by regenerative science.

Daniele’s work brings together medicine, thoracic research, chemistry and tissue engineering around clinically meaningful questions in lung transplantation.

Daniele Evangelista is a Postdoctoral Research Fellow in Lung Transplantation at Toronto General Hospital, University Health Network, working under the mentorship of Dr. Shaf Keshavjee in the Latner Thoracic Research Laboratories.

She earned an MD from the University of Campinas and a PhD from the University of São Paulo. Her doctoral research investigated decellularized lung extracellular-matrix hydrogels as a therapeutic strategy in models of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis.

Her current program uses ex vivo lung perfusion as a therapeutic window: a controlled period in which donor lungs can be evaluated, supported and potentially treated before transplantation.

Research program

One translational question, four connected research layers.

How can a donor lung be assessed and treated more precisely before it reaches the recipient?

01

Targeted mRNA delivery

Developing cell-directed strategies that use click chemistry and lipid nanoparticles to deliver temporary genetic instructions during EVLP.

  • Cell-selective targeting
  • Transient therapeutic payloads
  • Delivery and safety assessment
02

Ex vivo lung perfusion

Using a functioning organ platform to observe physiology, deliver therapy and measure response before implantation.

  • Controlled perfusion and ventilation
  • Real-time organ assessment
  • Pre-transplant intervention
03

Regenerative biomaterials

Investigating extracellular-matrix-derived hydrogels and the signals they provide in injury, repair and fibrotic remodeling.

  • Decellularized lung matrix
  • Hydrogel characterization
  • Pulmonary fibrosis mechanisms
04

Clinical translation

Connecting mechanism, reproducibility and organ-level evidence to the practical requirements of transplantation.

  • Clinically relevant models
  • Multidisciplinary collaboration
  • Patient-centred outcomes
2026 ISHLT Research Fellowship

Click Chemistry for Targeted mRNA Delivery During Ex Vivo Lung Perfusion

A novel strategy to improve donor-lung quality.

The project develops a targeted approach for delivering mRNA-carrying lipid nanoparticles directly to selected cells during EVLP. The goal is to enable precise intervention where it is needed most, support lung repair, reduce early graft dysfunction and increase the number of lungs suitable for transplantation.
