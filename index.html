Daniele Evangelista is a physician-scientist working at the intersection of lung transplantation, molecular delivery, tissue engineering and regenerative medicine.

At Toronto General Hospital within University Health Network, she is a postdoctoral research fellow in the Latner Thoracic Research Laboratories. Her current work uses ex vivo lung perfusion as a controlled platform for evaluating targeted treatment before transplantation.

Her doctoral research investigated decellularized lung extracellular-matrix hydrogel in models of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis. Earlier organ-engineering work at Massachusetts General Hospital contributed to studies in lung regeneration, scaffold functionalization and composite-tissue engineering.

Bring chemistry, engineering and transplantation together to address clinically meaningful problems.

Organ intervention

Turn preservation time into treatment time.

Ex vivo lung perfusion maintains a donor lung outside the body in a controlled, functioning state, creating a window for physiological assessment and direct intervention before implantation.

1Observe lung function under controlled perfusion and ventilation.
2Apply treatment directly to the donor organ before transplantation.
3Measure physiological and molecular response during support.

Click Chemistry for Targeted mRNA Delivery During Ex Vivo Lung Perfusion

A novel strategy to improve donor lung quality.

The project develops a targeted way to deliver mRNA-carrying lipid nanoparticles during EVLP. Click chemistry is used to guide the carrier toward selected cells, enabling treatment before transplantation while the lung is supported outside the body.
