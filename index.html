About

Clinical questions.
Engineering solutions.

A medical perspective shaped by chemistry, tissue engineering and regenerative science.

Daniele Evangelista is a physician-scientist working at the intersection of lung transplantation, molecular delivery, tissue engineering and regenerative medicine.

At Toronto General Hospital within University Health Network, she works under the mentorship of Dr. Shaf Keshavjee in the Latner Thoracic Research Laboratories. Her current program uses ex vivo lung perfusion as a controlled window to evaluate and treat donor lungs before implantation.

Her medical and doctoral training connects clinical perspective with chemistry and extracellular-matrix biology. Her PhD research investigated decellularized lung extracellular-matrix hydrogels in models of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis.

Bring chemistry, engineering and transplantation together to address clinically meaningful problems.

Research platform

Four connected layers of translational lung science.

Each area contributes a distinct piece of the pathway from molecular targeting to organ-level intervention.

Precision therapeutic delivery

Guide the payload to the intended cell.

Click chemistry is being investigated as a strategy to guide mRNA-carrying lipid nanoparticles toward selected cell populations in donor lungs.

1Use transient mRNA instructions rather than permanent genetic modification.
2Improve cellular specificity through selective molecular docking.
3Evaluate delivery in an organ-level system relevant to transplantation.

Organ intervention

Turn preservation time into treatment time.

Ex vivo lung perfusion maintains a donor lung outside the body in a controlled, functioning state. This creates a window for physiological assessment and direct treatment before implantation.

1Observe lung function under controlled perfusion and ventilation.
2Apply therapy directly to the donor organ before transplantation.
3Measure organ response in real time.

Regenerative biomaterials

Learn from the lung’s extracellular architecture.

Doctoral research evaluated decellularized lung extracellular-matrix hydrogels and biological pathways associated with inflammation, oxidative injury, tissue remodeling and fibrosis.

1Characterize matrix composition and hydrogel formation.
2Study inflammatory, oxidative and fibrotic pathways.
3Identify mechanisms that may support tissue repair.

Clinical translation

Design every experiment around a patient pathway.

Clinical questions, chemical tools and engineering methods are integrated to develop interventions that can be tested in transplant-relevant systems.

1Start with a defined transplant bottleneck.
2Build a measurable mechanism around that need.
3Evaluate safety, feasibility and organ-level response.

Click Chemistry for Targeted mRNA Delivery During Ex Vivo Lung Perfusion

A novel strategy to improve donor lung quality.

The project develops a targeted way to deliver mRNA-carrying lipid nanoparticles during EVLP. Click chemistry is used to guide the therapeutic carrier toward selected cells, enabling treatment before transplantation while the lung is supported outside the body.
